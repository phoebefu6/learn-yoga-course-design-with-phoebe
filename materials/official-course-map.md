# Official course map - learn-yoga-course-design-with-phoebe

Internal build document (never linked from an audience page). Every number a page quotes must be
in this file with its tier. Full quotes and citations: `materials/research-notes.md` (read
2026-10-12). Bench canon: `materials/yc_reference.py` and `materials/yc-node.js` (byte-identical
JSON, checked 2026-10-12).

Bucket `move`, d3, single-track 6 x 45 min, audience everyone (aspiring and new yoga teachers).
Running case: **Juniper Studio** (constructed): a community studio commissioning an 8-week beginner
programme, one 60-minute class a week, 12 students, mixed ages; cohort flags wrist 3, knee 2,
balance 1, back 1. Every page labels it constructed. Not medical advice: said on the landing page,
session 1 and the bench.

## Verified facts by source (tier, where used)

### Trial programmes (s1, s6)
- Sherman 2011 Arch Intern Med 171(22):2019-26 (A): 228 adults (yoga 92, stretching 91, book 45);
  12 weekly 75-minute classes; "Six distinct and progressive classes were taught in pairs"; 17
  relatively simple postures, 5 to 11 per class; 20 minutes home practice on non-class days.
  Function at 12 weeks vs book: -2.5 (95% CI -3.7 to -1.3) on the 23-point modified Roland scale.
  Yoga not superior to stretching at any point. 65% of yoga attended at least 8 classes (stretching
  59%). Median home practice 100 min/week at week 6, 60 min at week 12.
- Tilbrook 2011 Ann Intern Med 155(9):569-78 (A abstract): 313 adults (156 yoga, 157 usual care);
  "a 12-class, gradually progressing yoga program delivered by 12 teachers over 3 months"; 60%
  (93) met the attendance bar (3 of first 6 plus 3 others); RMDQ 2.17 points better at 3 months
  (95% CI 1.03 to 3.31), 1.48 at 6, 1.57 at 12 months; adverse events 12 of 156 vs 2 of 157,
  mostly increased pain. Class length: R, do not quote.
- Saper 2017 Ann Intern Med 167(2):85-94 (A): 320 adults, predominantly low-income and racially
  diverse (yoga 127, PT 129, education 64); 12 weekly 75-minute classes; props (chair, strap,
  blocks); ratio under 5:1; 30 min daily home practice encouraged; yoga noninferior to PT; median
  attendance 7 classes (IQR 3 to 10); 44% (56) attended 9 or more; home practice reported by 75%,
  median 27 min, 4 days/week; maintenance drop-in vs home practice made no difference at 52 weeks.
- Zhang 2021 J Altern Complement Med 27(5):442-57 (A): 22 tai chi, qigong and yoga interventions
  scored on CERT; a progression rule was reported by 1 (5%); starting level rule 1 (5%).
- Cramer 2016 eCAM 2016:5859729 (A): 168 yoga RCTs, dropout 11.42% (95% CI 10.11 to 12.73) vs
  14.53% in exercise controls; above 12 weeks 15.23%.
- Denham-Jones 2022 Musculoskeletal Care 20(1):47-73 (A): 11 studies, n = 2221 older adults;
  effective trials used at least one 60-min group class and on average four 30-min home sessions
  weekly.

### Intake and safety (s2)
- PAR-Q+ (A, eparmedx.com): pre-participation screening; page 1 has seven questions; any YES routes
  to follow-up (ePARmed-X+); current official form is the 2025 edition. Exact wording of the seven
  questions: R. Status "international standard": A (Schwartz 2021 Front Cardiovasc Med 8:712696).
- Riebe 2015 MSSE 47(11):2473-9 (A): ACSM algorithm uses (1) current physical activity, (2) signs or
  symptoms and/or known cardiovascular, metabolic or renal disease, (3) desired exercise intensity.
  Old screening "can result in excessive physician referrals, possibly creating a barrier".
  Risk-factor profiling dropped.
- Cramer 2015 Am J Epidemiol 182(4):281-93 (A): of 301 yoga RCTs, 94 (8,430 participants) reported
  adverse events; no difference vs usual care or exercise; "yoga appears as safe as usual care and
  exercise".
- Swain and McGwin 2016 Orthop J Sports Med 4(11) (A): 29,590 estimated ED yoga injuries 2001-2014;
  trunk 46.6%; sprain/strain 45.0%; 2014 rate per 100,000 participants 57.9 at 65+, 17.7 at 45-64,
  11.9 at 18-44.
- Jasien 2015 PLoS One 10(12):e0144505 (A): 10 glaucoma + 10 normal, 2-minute holds; downward dog
  glaucoma 17 to 28 mmHg (normal 17 to 29); standing forward fold 17 to 27 (normal 18 to 26);
  plough 18 to 24 (18 to 22); legs up the wall 17 to 21 (17 to 21); back to baseline within 2 min
  of sitting up; risk to progression not tested.
- Yoga Alliance Scope of Practice, updated 27 Feb 2020 (A): "Members must not provide advice or
  services for which they are not properly and currently licensed"; refer, gain credentials or
  decline; consent for touch, "Previous consent does not imply future permission". The words
  diagnose/treat are NOT on this page; they are in the Yoga Therapy Policy, updated 18 Nov 2021
  (A): Yoga Alliance does "not credential yoga therapy techniques, including the diagnosis or
  treatment".
- Yoga Alliance RYS 200 standards effective 27 Feb 2020 (A): four categories, Professional
  Essentials includes sequencing; designing a multi-week programme is not named as a competency.

### Progression and periodisation (s3)
- ACSM 2009 MSSE 41(3):687-708 (A abstract): "2-10% increase in load be applied when the individual
  can perform the current workload for one to two repetitions over the desired number"; apply "in
  context". The number is for external load; transfer to yoga is our inference, label it.
- Garber 2011 MSSE 43(7):1334-59 (A abstract): flexibility "a total of 60 s per exercise" on 2 or
  more days a week; neuromotor exercise (balance, agility, coordination) 2-3 days a week; "gradual
  progression of exercise intensity and volume may reduce the risks". 10-30 s hold: R, do not quote.
- Williams 2017 Sports Med 47(10):2083-2100 (A): 18 studies, 81 effects; periodised > non-periodised
  for maximal strength, ES 0.43 (95% CI 0.27 to 0.58); undulating models more favourable.
- Afonso 2019 Front Physiol 10:1023 (A): "None of the 21 studies included in the two meta-analyses
  compared periodized programs with varied, non-periodized programs."
- Kiely 2012 IJSPP 7(3):242-50 (A): training responses vary widely; not a rejection of periodisation;
  argues for responsive systems. Kiely 2018 Sports Med 48(4):753-64 (A): its foundation (Selye's
  stress model) needs re-evaluating.
- Anyadike-Danes 2023 Sports Med Open 9:109 (A): 106 coaches; 71% call their approach periodisation;
  33% agree adaptations are achievable in fixed timeframes; 10% support fixed targets.
- Thomas 2018 IJSM 39(4):243-54 (A): weekly stretching time matters at 5 min or more a week; vs
  Konrad 2024 JSHS 13(2):186-94 (A): 77 studies, volume, intensity and frequency made no
  significant difference. Konrad 2024 Sports Med 54(9) (A): static stretching ROM gains already
  significant in programmes of 4 weeks or less (ES -1.436, paper's sign convention).

### Repetition and spacing for skills (s3)
- Shea and Morgan 1979 (A abstract): random practice retained and transferred better than blocked.
  The "blocked looks better during acquisition" half: R (reviews), say reported.
- Brady 2004 Percept Mot Skills 99(1):116-26 (A): 61 studies, 139 effects, overall 0.38; basic
  research 0.57, applied 0.19; adults 0.50, younger learners 0.10. Brady 2008 (A): "considerably
  weaker in applied settings".
- Guadagnoli and Lee 2004 J Mot Behav 36(2):212-24 (A): challenge point; the right difficulty
  depends on the performer's skill. No effect sizes.
- Shea 2000 (spacing across days), Magill and Hall 1990: X. Title only, no numbers.
- Block new poses first, interleave once familiar: our inference from Brady + challenge point,
  label it as an inference, not a yoga finding.

### Mixed-level rooms (s5)
- Deunk 2018 Educ Res Rev 24:31-54 (A abstract): 21 studies, 78 effects in primary maths and
  language; differentiation within broader programmes small to moderate positive; homogeneous
  ability grouping small negative effect for low-ability students, none for others.
- Smale-Jacobse 2019 Front Psychol 10:2366 (A): 14 papers on 12 studies in secondary school;
  effects d = 0.741 to 0.509 omitting an outlier; "severe knowledge gaps". Both are school
  academic outcomes; transfer to a yoga room is an inference.
- CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0, 30 July 2024 (A): three principles (Engagement, Representation, Action
  and Expression), nine guidelines, 36 considerations. A design framework, not an outcome trial.
- Saper props and ratio (above).

### Finishing well (s6)
- Lally 2010 Eur J Soc Psychol 40(6):998-1009 (A abstract): 96 volunteers, 82 analysed; time to 95%
  of the automaticity plateau ranged 18 to 254 days; missing one day did not materially affect it.
  66-day median: R. "21 days" (Maltz 1960): R, no peer-reviewed source found.
- NHS Back pain page, reviewed 5 March 2026 (A): see a GP if pain does not improve after a few
  weeks, is worse at night, came with unexplained weight loss; urgent GP or 111 if severe pain
  starts suddenly or worsens quickly, or the person feels hot, shivery or unwell; 999 or A&E for
  numbness, tingling or weakness in both legs, loss of feeling around the genitals or anus, bladder
  or bowel changes, chest pain, or pain after a serious accident.
- NHS yoga safety page: X (now resolves to a general exercise page). Never cite as NHS guidance.
- Trials measured function with clinical scales (Roland); a teacher tracks what students can do
  and how confident they feel, never a clinical score. Practitioner convention, constructed
  examples.

## Sessions

1. **A programme is not eight classes in a row** (`01-a-programme-not-eight-classes.html`,
   foundations). Parts: what three trials ran (Sherman, Tilbrook, Saper); attendance and home
   practice (65%, 60%, median 7 of 12; 100 to 60 min); the four-phase arc foundation, build,
   integrate, consolidate (practitioner convention, constructed for Juniper) plus Zhang 5%.
   Build-along: Juniper's programme charter.
2. **Intake: who is in the room** (`02-intake-who-is-in-the-room.html`, foundations). Parts: PAR-Q+
   and the ACSM 2015 three factors (over-referral is a barrier); caution flags as referral not
   diagnosis (Jasien, Swain and McGwin, Cramer 2015); scope and consent (Yoga Alliance). Build-along:
   Juniper's intake form and flag map (12 students, wrist 3, knee 2, balance 1, back 1).
3. **Progressions across weeks** (`03-progressions-across-weeks.html`, core). Parts: three levers
   load, complexity, hold time (ACSM 2009 principle, Garber 60 s, Thomas vs Konrad); periodisation
   dispute (Williams 0.43 vs Afonso, Kiely, coach survey); block then interleave (Shea and Morgan,
   Brady 0.38 / 0.19, challenge point). Build-along: three pose-family progressions over 8 weeks,
   stick-figure guides with safe range and stop-if.
4. **The programme bench** (`04-the-programme-bench.html`, bench). Canon above.
5. **One room, many levels** (`05-one-room-many-levels.html`, deeper). Parts: what differentiation
   research shows (Deunk, Smale-Jacobse; ability grouping caveat); options ladders and props (UDL
   3.0, Saper); offering choice without ranking (practitioner convention) and the late joiner.
   Build-along: Juniper week 5 with a three-option card per pose.
6. **Finishing well, and what comes next** (`06-finishing-well.html`, deeper). Parts: progress
   without diagnosing (trial scales vs teacher markers); home practice fades and habits take time
   (Sherman 100 to 60, Saper maintenance, Lally 18-254, Cramer dropout); when to refer (NHS back
   pain triggers, scope of practice). Build-along: week 8 plan plus a handover note per student.

## Not covered (honest list)

- Sequencing a single class, cueing, anatomy, pranayama, philosophy: Yoga Theory.
- Prenatal, children's, chair-only or clinical yoga therapy programmes; therapeutic dosing for any
  condition. Yoga therapy is a separate credential.
- Business side: pricing, marketing, booking systems.
- Legal and insurance requirements, which differ by country.
- Any claim that a programme treats back pain or another condition: the trials compared yoga
  with other care; these pages use them only for how programmes were built and attended.
- Exact PAR-Q+ question wording (read the official 2025 form at eparmedx.com).
- Optimal session spacing for motor skills (Shea 2000 not read).
