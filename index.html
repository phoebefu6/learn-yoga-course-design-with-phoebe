The six sessions 🧘 · from a brief to a programme that hands students on

The running case is Juniper Studio, a constructed community studio that has asked a new teacher for an 8-week beginner programme: one 60-minute class a week, twelve students, mixed ages, three with sore wrists. Juniper, its students and every number about them are invented for teaching, and every page says so.

Not medical advice. These pages are about planning a programme. They do not teach anyone to diagnose, treat or clear a student for exercise. A student with chest pain, dizziness, a recent injury, pain that worsens with movement or a condition their doctor has told them to manage belongs with a clinician first. Session 2 builds the intake and session 6 lists the warning signs.

What is honest here 🧾

Programme design borrows from training science and learning research, and some of what it borrows is disputed. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus
Every bench figure: prerequisite gaps, load spikes, lighter weeks, one-off poses, regression offersModelled in your browser from a constructed 42-pose library and 1 to 5 teaching levels; nothing measures a real student
"Periodised training beats non-periodised training"Contested. A 2017 meta-analysis found an advantage for strength; a 2019 review found none of its studies compared against varied training. Session 3
"Yoga is safe"As safe as usual care and exercise in trials, but only 94 of 301 trials reported adverse events at all. Session 2
"Students will come every week"No. In a 12-week trial in a low-income sample the median attendance was 7 classes. Session 1
"A habit takes 21 days"No peer-reviewed source found. One study of everyday habits found the time to automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days. Session 6
