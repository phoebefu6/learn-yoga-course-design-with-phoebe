A programme plan is a grid of weeks and poses, and its mistakes hide in the grid: a pose taught before its foundation, a week that jumps two levels, a pose taught once and never again. Part 1 · covers the model

Five checks you could do by hand

5 min live

The bench is simple on purpose, so you can check any number on paper. A plan is 8 weekly classes, each an ordered list of poses. Every pose in the constructed library has a load level and a complexity level from 1 to 5, a list of poses it needs first, and caution flags such as wrist or knee. Five checks run over the grid.

LiveThe rules, so you can check any number by hand3 min▶

Prerequisite gap: a pose whose prerequisite was not taught in an earlier week, or earlier in the same class. Load spike: a week whose top load is 2 or more levels above anything the group has met so far; the group starts able to meet level 2. Lighter week: a week whose total load (the sum of its poses' levels) is lower than the week before. One-off pose: taught in exactly one week. Spaced pose: taught in 3 or more weeks. Regression offers: for each pose, the number of students in the cohort whose flag it carries, added up over the 8 weeks.

The cohort is Juniper's twelve constructed students: 3 with a wrist flag, 2 knee, 1 balance, 1 back, and 5 with none. A regression offer is not a problem; it is a prepared option you will need, and the number tells you how often.

Self-studyWhat the model leaves out, on purpose2 min▶

Load and complexity levels are teaching judgements, not measurements; two experienced teachers might score a pose one level apart. The bench ignores order within a class beyond prerequisites, hold times, breathing and the shape of each class, which belong to Yoga Theory session 7 ↗. It treats a pose taught in a week as learned, which is generous. It is a structural check, the way a spell checker is a writing check: it catches a class of mistake reliably and says nothing about whether the plan is beautiful.

Build-along · the bench

Juniper's 8 weeks, rung by rung

★ 26 min · the main event

Start on rung 1 and read the verdict line, then the eight numbers, then the chart and the grid. Move one rung at a time and say which number moved and which way. Only then press the two red buttons, one at a time, on rung 4. Last, switch to "My own plan", type your 8 weeks, and run it.

Read it in this order. Prerequisite gaps, load spikes, one-off poses, then complexity. On rung 1, eleven poses drawn at random each week (seed 7) give 29 prerequisite gaps, 1 load spike and 8 one-off poses across 35 different poses, and complexity drifts from 2.7 in week 1 to 2.3 in week 8: a lot of material, no direction.

Part 2 · covers the ladder

Each rung fixes one thing and exposes the next

5 min live

No rung is perfect, and that is the lesson. Reading the four side by side shows what each plan shape is good at and what it costs.

LiveRungs 2 and 3: the two honest mistakes3 min▶

Rung 2 repeats the week 1 foundation class eight times. Every check passes: no gaps, no spikes, no one-offs, all 11 poses spaced. And complexity sits at 1.3 from week 1 to week 8, with a top load of 3. Nothing is wrong and nothing changes; Mei's students would leave able to do exactly what they did on night one. A clean check is not the same as a programme.

Rung 3 climbs every week: complexity rises from 1.3 to 3.9, week loads go 15, 22, 29, 33, 39, 40, 45, 44, and it reaches load 5 poses by week 5. It respects every prerequisite. But it rotates so fast that 15 of its 37 poses are taught in a single week, the opposite of what session 1 found in the trials, and fatal for anyone who misses a class. Its only lighter week is week 8, by one point.

LiveRung 4: slower on purpose3 min▶

The wave plan climbs less: complexity from 1.3 to 2.1, peaking at 2.2, and its top load never passes 4. In exchange, 23 of its 25 poses come back in three or more weeks, none is a one-off, and weeks 4 and 7 are lighter (loads 18 and 21 against 28 and 35 before them). For beginners who will come to about seven of twelve classes, that trade is usually right. A programme for experienced students could climb faster; the bench lets you see the cost when you do.

Watch the regression offers too: 119 for rung 4, against 146 for rung 3, because rung 3's later weeks are full of poses that load the wrists and put the head below the heart.

Real world

The beginner series that sold out and did not rebook. A familiar studio story: an ambitious six-week series, a new challenge every week, great energy in week 1, half the faces gone by week 4. Each missed week left a student further behind a plan that never looked back. Rung 3 is that series in numbers. The fix is not easier poses; it is coming back to the ones already taught.

Part 3 · covers the anti-lever and the break button

The peak pose every week, and the student in week 5

5 min live

Two buttons, two lessons. The first feels like good teaching and quietly breaks the plan. The second is ordinary studio life, and the plan either has an answer ready or it does not.

LiveWhy a peak pose every week breaks a beginner programme3 min▶

Building one class toward a peak pose is a respected method, taught in Yoga Theory session 7 ↗, where the class itself prepares the peak. Turn it into a weekly habit across a beginner programme and the peaks start arriving before the weeks that would have prepared them. Press the button on rung 4: crow closes week 1 without the garland squat or plank it needs, wheel closes week 2 before upward dog, and headstand closes week 3 before dolphin. Gaps go from 0 to 3, crow is a load spike on night one, one-off poses go from 0 to 7 because each showcase appears once, and the top load reaches 5.

From week 4 the gaps stop, because by then the plan's own weeks have taught what the later showcases need. The damage is already done in the weeks where beginners are most likely to decide yoga is not for them.

LiveThe late joiner3 min▶

Now clear the first button and press the second. A student joins in week 5 whose doctor has advised them to avoid positions with the head below the heart; session 2 shows why such advice exists for some eye conditions. You do not need to know their condition, and you should not ask for more than they offer. You need to know which planned poses put the head below the heart from week 5 on. On rung 4 the bench lists three: downward dog in weeks 5, 6 and 8, each with its prepared option, hands on a wall or a chair seat. Regression offers rise from 119 to 122.

Switch to rung 3 with the button still on: nine poses, including dolphin three times, crow three times and headstand twice. The plan that climbs fastest is the one least ready for the room changing. On rung 2 the number is 0, because the foundation class has no head-below-heart pose at all.

Self-studyRun your own plan and read three things4 min▶

Switch to "My own plan". The box starts as a copy of rung 4; replace it with your 8 weeks, one line per week, pose codes or names separated by commas (the library table under the widget lists every code). Then read three things. First, the gaps: for each "!" in the grid, either move the pose later or teach its prerequisite earlier. Second, the one-offs: each rose row is a pose a student who misses that week never meets. Third, press the break button and count how many regressions you need ready from week 5. Write the three numbers down for session 5.

Homework

Before session 5

20 min
  1. Get your plan to zero gaps. Run it, fix every "!", run it again. Note how many poses you had to move.
  2. Halve your one-offs. For each pose taught once, either bring it back in a neighbouring week or cut it. Which did you choose more often, and why?
  3. Find your lighter weeks. If your plan has none, choose two weeks to lighten and say what students consolidate in them.
  4. Prepare the late joiner's card. Press the break button on your plan and write the regression you would offer for each listed pose, in words a student would understand.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 4 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Rung 2 repeats the foundation class eight times and passes every check: no gaps, no spikes, no one-off poses. What does the bench show is wrong with it?

Clean checks are necessary, not sufficient. Rung 2 is safe and static; students would leave able to do exactly what they did on night one.

2 · On rung 4, what does the "peak pose every week" anti-lever do?

Each showcase arrives before the weeks that would have prepared it, and each appears once. The peak-pose method works inside one class that prepares the peak; as a weekly habit in a beginner programme it jumps ahead of the foundations.

3 · A student whose doctor advised avoiding head-below-heart positions joins in week 5. What is the teacher's job, and what does rung 4 need?

A referral flag means you adapt and suggest they keep their clinician informed; it never means you assess the condition. Rung 4 needs 3 ready regressions; the faster-climbing rung 3 would need 9.
