Part 0

Where we start

Juniper Studio (constructed) is two weeks from the end of its programme. Mei asks two things: "How do we show people they have improved?" and "What do we tell them on the last night?" Both are easy to answer badly. A before-and-after flexibility test turns a yoga class into an assessment nobody consented to, and "see you around" sends people nowhere. This session answers both properly.

Not medical advice. The referral signs below come from the NHS back pain page and are there so you know when to stop and send someone on, not so you can judge what is wrong. Outside the UK, use your own health service's equivalents.

Part 1 · covers what trials measured and what a teacher tracks

Progress you can see, without a diagnosis

5 min live

The trials from session 1 measured progress with clinical scales. Sherman's team used a 23-point modified Roland scale of back-related function; Tilbrook's reported 2.17 points of difference on the Roland Morris questionnaire at 3 months. Those instruments are about a condition, scored by researchers under ethics approval. A teacher's version of progress has to be something else.

LiveTrack the charter, not the body3 min▶

Session 1's charter already wrote the markers: by week 8 a student can move through a short standing sequence from memory, choose a regression that suits them, come down to the floor and up safely, and name two poses they will keep. Those are observable skills. Turn them into a short self-check the student fills in at week 1 and week 8, keeps, and never hands in. Add one number they own: confidence from 0 to 10.

What stays out: pain scores, before-and-after reach tests, anything that measures a condition. Range of motion does change with regular stretching, and a 2024 meta-analysis found gains already significant in programmes of four weeks or less, but the moment a teacher records it as a result, the class has become an assessment of someone's body. If a student wants that kind of measurement, it belongs with a physiotherapist.

Self-studyMeasuring change is its own craft2 min▶

How to measure whether any learning programme changed behaviour, and why self-reports flatter, is covered in Course Design: measuring behaviour change ↗. The yoga-specific point is narrower: keep every marker about what the student can do and how they feel about doing it, and let the student hold the record. Nothing you need for a good programme requires you to store health information after the last class.

Real world

The before-and-after photo. Some beginner courses photograph students in a forward fold in week 1 and week 8 to show progress. It looks motivating on social media. For the student who could not reach further, it is a public record of not improving, taken in a position they did not choose. A private self-check card does the motivating part without the exposure.

Part 2 · covers home practice, dropout and Lally 2010

Practice fades, and habits take longer than 21 days

5 min live

The programme ends; the practice is supposed to continue. The evidence says it mostly fades unless something carries it, and that the popular timeline for habits has no good source.

LiveWhat the trials saw after the classes3 min▶

Sherman's yoga students practised a median of 100 minutes a week at home by week 6 and 60 by week 12, while the programme was still running. Saper's trial then tested what happened next: for a year after the 12 weeks, some yoga participants were offered drop-in classes and others home practice, and the two groups did not differ in function or pain at 52 weeks. Among older adults with musculoskeletal conditions, a 2022 review found effective trials used at least one 60-minute class a week plus, on average, four 30-minute home sessions.

Dropout is a separate number. A 2016 review of 168 yoga trials found an average dropout of 11.42 percent in yoga groups, rising to 15.23 percent in programmes longer than 12 weeks. People who stay enrolled still miss classes and still let home practice slide.

LiveThe 21-day myth and what Lally actually found3 min▶

"It takes 21 days to form a habit" is usually traced to Maxwell Maltz's 1960 book Psycho-Cybernetics, which was about adjusting to changes in self-image (reported; the book was not read). No peer-reviewed source for 21 days was found. Phillippa Lally's team followed 96 volunteers who each picked one daily eating, drinking or activity behaviour. The time to reach the plateau of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days, and missing one opportunity did not materially affect the process. A median of 66 days is widely quoted from the full paper; it was not read at source here, so treat it as reported.

For a programme designer the range is the lesson. Day 56 is the last class of an 8-week programme, and many students will be nowhere near automatic. A short, fixed home sequence tied to an existing daily cue gives the habit a chance to finish forming after you have gone. Habit cues for desk breaks are taught next door in Office Energy ↗.

Real world

The 30-day challenge. Studios often run 30-day challenges with a certificate at the end. They can be fun. On Lally's range, day 30 is early for most people, and missing a day is not the failure a streak counter makes it look. A challenge that says "missing a day is fine, carry on" fits the evidence better than one that resets to zero.

Part 3 · covers the NHS back pain page and the Yoga Alliance scope

Refer out, hand on

5 min live

Two kinds of goodbye happen at the end of a programme. Most students move on to more yoga. A few should move to a clinician, and some of them will mention it on the last night. The teacher's job is to recognise the second kind and route it, not to work out what is wrong.

LiveSaying it without diagnosing3 min▶

The Yoga Alliance Scope of Practice says members "must not provide advice or services for which they are not properly and currently licensed," and that members may give students referral lists of local resources, including medical and mental health professionals. In practice that means three sentences: what you noticed ("you mentioned the pain wakes you at night"), what you are not able to judge ("that is not something I can assess"), and where to go ("that is worth a GP appointment before next week"). Never add a guess about the cause.

If someone describes the 999 signs in class, such as new numbness in both legs or chest pain, that is not a conversation for after class. Stop, and get emergency help.

Self-studyHanding on the rest of the room2 min▶

For everyone else, the handover is a next step with a date on it: the studio's open beginner class, the next run of the programme, or the home sequence. Some students will want a different practice altogether; Tai Chi ↗ and Meditation ↗ are good onward doors. A student who leaves with a named next class is far more likely to keep going than one who leaves with "keep it up".

Keep the referral list ready from week 1. A short printed list of local GPs, physiotherapists and the urgent numbers, kept with your class register, means you never improvise this conversation. It is also what the Yoga Alliance scope explicitly allows you to share.

Build-along

Week 8 and the handover

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Juniper's week 8 (constructed) is the consolidation class from the wave plan you ran on the bench. Nothing in it is new: every pose was first taught in an earlier week, so the class becomes a chance for students to notice what they now own. Then each student leaves with a self-check, a home sequence and a next step.

Confirm week 8 adds nothing new. Check every pose against the week it was first taught, as the figure does. If your own week 8 has a new pose, move it earlier or cut it; the last night is for consolidation.

★ Step 1 · Juniper week 8 (constructed)Mountain, Chair, High lunge, Warrior I, Extended side angle, Tree, Warrior III, Downward dog, Plank, Locust, Pigeon, Seated twist, Rest New this week: none. Newest: weeks 5 and 6.

Write the student self-check. The same card at week 1 and week 8. The student fills it in and keeps it. You never collect it.

★ Step 2 · the self-check cardWeek 1 / Week 8 (circle one) [ ] I can move through the standing flow from memory [ ] I can choose an option that suits me in any pose [ ] I can get down to the floor and up again safely Confidence coming to an open class today: __ / 10 Two poses I will keep practising: ________ ________

Choose a five-pose home sequence. Only poses every student has met at least three times, each with the option they can do without you. Keep it short enough to do on the worst day of the week.

★ Step 3 · home sequence, about 10 minutes1 Mountain, 5 breaths 2 Tree, 5 breaths each side (toes down, hand on a wall) 3 Downward dog, 5 breaths (or hands on a chair seat) 4 Low cobra, 3 rounds (or sphinx) 5 Rest, 2 minutes Stop if anything is painful, dizzying or numb.

Write the handover note. One per student, three lines, no health details: what they did well, the next step with a date, and the home sequence. Anyone who mentioned a referral sign gets the referral sentence instead of a pose suggestion.

★ Step 4 · handover templateName: ________ You did well with: ________________________ Your next step: open beginner class, Tuesdays 18:00, from ____ At home: the five-pose sequence on the back of this card.

Put the referral list on the back of your register. From Part 3: GP, physiotherapy, urgent line, emergency number, in your country's terms. You share it; you do not interpret symptoms.

★ Step 5 · referral list (UK example)GP surgery: ________ Physiotherapy: ________ Urgent advice: NHS 111 Emergency: 999 Script: "That is not something I can assess. It is worth seeing your GP before next week."

Now do your own. Take the plan you ran on the bench in session 4. Check week 8 for new poses, write your self-check from your session 1 charter, pick your five home poses and your next-step class. That is the whole programme, from brief to handover.

★ Step 6 · your finishWeek 8 new poses: ____ (target: none) Self-check items, from my charter: 1 ____ 2 ____ 3 ____ Home sequence: ____, ____, ____, ____, ____ Next step and date: ____________________

End on what they own. Close the last class by asking students to look at their week 1 card next to their week 8 card, privately. The change they see themselves is worth more than anything you could tell them.

Homework

After the last session

30 min
  1. Assemble the programme pack. Charter (session 1), intake and flag map (2), progressions (3), bench-checked plan (4), options cards (5), and tonight's week 8, self-check and handover. One folder.
  2. Run the plan one last time on the bench. Zero prerequisite gaps, no load spikes, as few one-offs as you can, and a list of regressions for a late joiner.
  3. Test your referral script out loud. Say the three sentences from Part 3 to a friend. If you hear yourself guessing at a cause, cut that sentence.
  4. Find one real next step. A class, a teacher or a programme near you that your students could go to after week 8. Write it on the handover template.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Mei wants to show Juniper's students they have improved. Which approach stays within a teacher's role?

Clinical scales and body measurements are about a condition and belong with researchers and clinicians. Skills the student can observe, and confidence they rate themselves, track the charter without assessing anyone's body.

2 · What did Lally and colleagues (2010) find about how long habits take?

The 21-day figure has no peer-reviewed source found; it is usually traced to a 1960 book about self-image. An 8-week programme ends at day 56, so the home sequence and handover have to carry the practice on.

3 · On the last night a student says their back pain now wakes them at night and has not improved for weeks. What should the teacher do?

Pain that is worse at night or not improving after a few weeks is on the NHS list of reasons to see a GP. The Yoga Alliance scope says not to advise beyond your licence, and allows sharing referral lists. The teacher routes; the clinician assesses.
